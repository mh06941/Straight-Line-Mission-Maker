# Geographic model and routing

## Coordinates and accuracy

Canonical input/output coordinates are WGS84 latitude/longitude; GeoJSON uses longitude/latitude. PyProj's ellipsoidal `Geod` provides inverse/direct distance and bearings. The ideal reference is the finite shortest geodesic, densified to at most 25 m segments. Exported GPX longitude is normalised to −180…180; map geometry is unwrapped around the mission for date-line rendering.

Local operations use an **azimuthal equidistant projection centered on the geodesic midpoint**. The ideal geodesic passes through its center and becomes a straight radial segment. This avoids UTM zone discontinuities and supplies a metric CRS in polar regions. It is preferable here to choosing one UTM zone for a cross-zone mission. The default analysis cap is 25 km; do not assume the same distortion/error budget after increasing that limit. OSM's raster basemap is Web Mercator, with no coverage beyond approximately 85.05° latitude, although backend calculations support the poles.

Overpass queries use the envelope of the geodesic buffer. Date-line envelopes are split, and buffers containing a pole use a full-longitude cap. Query area is integrated on the WGS84 ellipsoid between parallels, avoiding zero-area polar boxes and underestimated large geographic rectangles.

## Access and topology

A direct Overpass-to-NetworkX adapter replaces OSMnx in this MVP: it retrieves obstacles and node-preserving walking geometry in one bounded request, keeps the access policy explicit, and avoids a second provider download. GeoPandas is unnecessary for the current geometry-sized operations; Shapely and PyProj supply them directly.

The graph is a NetworkX undirected multigraph built directly from Overpass way node IDs. Adjacent way nodes form edges; parallel ways remain distinct. A geometric crossing never becomes a mapped intersection by proximity. Shared OSM nodes provide mapped connectivity, preserving normal bridge/tunnel grade separation. Relaxed mode may add the separately labelled crossing proposals described below. OSM tagging errors remain possible. A Shapely spatial index also filters edges against mapped building/water/restricted interiors and fence/wall intersections; tagged bridges, tunnels, specific foot permissions and permitted gate locations provide conservative exceptions.

Explicit `foot=no/private` blocks an edge. Explicit foot permission can override general private vehicle/land access. `highway=footway/pedestrian/steps` supplies supporting pedestrian designation; generic `path` and roads without relevant access tags remain uncertain. Strict mode excludes uncertain edges. Conditional access and opening-hour tags remain uncertain because time-dependent evaluation is not implemented. Directional footways/conveying ways are conservatively omitted rather than routed against their restrictions. Locked or non-permitted barrier nodes remove adjacent edges. Lowered/flush/no kerbs are passable nodes on mapped pedestrian ways, preserving crossing connectivity. A kerb of unknown or raised height is admitted only in relaxed mode and generates an on-site check; explicit restrictions remain blocking.

Start and finish snap to the closest eligible edge within 100 m. That edge is split at the projected nearest point. **No access connector is invented**: the route begins at the snapped point; gap distance is displayed separately, and any gap above 1 m makes the whole mission status uncertain. Distance/deviation metrics cover only the generated path geometry.

Relaxed mode builds visibility meshes inside closed park/common/recreation polygons and `highway=pedestrian/footway + area=yes` plazas. Missing access tags remain uncertain; explicit restrictions exclude a space unless a specific foot permission overrides general access. Grade-separated spaces are omitted. A plaza's polygon boundary is not treated as a mapped walking path.

Up to 20 spaces nearest the reference line are considered. Each mesh retains at most 64 existing ground-level nodes, 128 points along the parts of the ideal line inside free ground, and 32 steering points around polygon/obstacle corners. Dense mapped spaces are sampled rather than discarded. Candidate links are at most 250 m and stay inside the space after subtraction of mapped buildings, water/fountains/pools, fences, railways, roads, restrictions and barrier nodes. Obstacles have a 0.5 m clearance buffer; steering points are offset inward a further 0.2 m. A 0.02 m visibility tolerance handles polygon-boundary projection round-off. Free-ground holes preserve fountain/pond avoidance; line points let the route return to the reference after a detour. These are bounded exploratory proposals, always marked uncertain. Complex multipolygon relations and generic farmland/forest access are not inferred.

Provider cache keys are versioned for the expanded selector set, so a region cached before fountain/pool retrieval is refreshed once. Fountain and swimming-pool polygons are treated as water obstacles in routing and analysis.

Current cache keys use the same six-decimal bounding coordinates as the actual Overpass query, while retaining reads of earlier version-2 keys. A downloaded complete StraightLine region bundle can be explicitly imported into this cache. Bounds, format, element IDs and fetch age are validated; the original fetch time and expiry are retained. Only matching query regions reuse the import, and saved missions/newer cache entries are preserved. This supplies actual downloaded data during a provider outage, not a synthetic or silently stale fallback.

Mapped pedestrian ways tagged `indoor=yes`, `highway=corridor` or `tunnel=building_passage` are eligible in relaxed mode and can pass through a building footprint. They remain uncertain and produce checks for entrances, opening hours and availability of through passage. Explicit foot restrictions and other mapped hazards still apply. No indoor route is invented from the building polygon alone.

With `allow_unverified_crossings=true` (the default, used only in relaxed mode), the model considers 2–60 m connections between ground-level mapped pedestrian nodes that cross eligible road geometry. It excludes links through mapped buildings, water/waterways, fences, restricted areas, locked barriers, active railways, motorways/trunks and grade-separated ways. Candidates are spatially indexed and bounded to 4,000 eligible nodes nearest the reference line and 1,000 proposals, ordered by maximum deviation then length. They are proposals, not mapped junctions or assertions that a road can be crossed at that point. Each used proposal is marked uncertain with a location and reason for an on-site check. Disable the crossing option to compare against the original network. These bounds mean the search is not exhaustive over all physically possible crossings.

## Bottleneck optimisation

Every edge's **full geometry** is geodesically densified at ≤5 m. Metric distance from every sample to the finite ideal geodesic is evaluated; the edge bottleneck uses its largest deviation plus a 0.05 m conservative guard. The UI reports a 0.5 m model numerical tolerance at the default local scale. This is a numerical modelling tolerance, not a positional accuracy claim about OSM or a proof about the physical path. Projected sampled segments approximate the tagged geodesic geometry; physical curvature not present in OSM cannot be recovered. Within a projected straight segment, distance to the reference segment is convex, so its maximum occurs at an endpoint. Sampling bounds curved projected geodesics, and the local cap controls distortion.

1. Bottleneck Dijkstra computes `b[v] = min(max(b[u], edge.deviation))`.
2. The user corridor is a target. If the optimal bottleneck exceeds it, **retain the best-effort mission route** with `status=outside_corridor`, `corridor_compliant=false` and `target_excess_m`. Preserve the original target and corridor geometry.
3. A second pass uses only edges at or below the optimal bottleneck (floating equality tolerance 1e−8 m). It minimises the additive integral of deviation over travelled distance, `∫ d(s) ds`, then total distance, then uncertain distance. The integral is computed from the same ≤5 m geodesic samples as other metrics using trapezoidal integration. Thus an unavoidable maximum elsewhere does not make a shorter but unnecessarily distant local detour preferable.

A single lexicographic Dijkstra pass does not always resolve a minimax route's additive secondary objective correctly: a prefix with a larger intermediate bottleneck may become preferable once both prefixes encounter a larger shared edge. The bottleneck bound is therefore solved first, then an additive lexicographic Dijkstra resolves accumulated deviation/distance/access costs inside that bound. Regression tests cover both the shared-bottleneck tie and preference for a longer, closer route. Accumulated deviation does not replace the primary maximum-deviation objective or alter the grading scale.

The conventional walking comparison uses shortest edge length through mapped ways, excluding synthetic open-space/crossing proposals and ignoring the target corridor. It can be unavailable where only a proposed connection completes a mission. Both route objectives operate on a finite local graph, not all possible real-world routes. Missing data/endpoints or a disconnected graph can still yield no route.

## Statistics and obstacles

Lengths use WGS84 geodesics. Average deviation integrates the trapezoidal sampled deviation weighted by travelled distance. Within-threshold percentages interpolate where sample deviations cross 10/25/50/100 m. OSM polyline curvature and access are only as complete as its mapping. Obstacle intersection positions and dimensions are local metric estimates. A road/water intersection remains a **mapped potential obstacle**, not a declaration of impassability. Possible crossing infrastructure within 25 m is labelled unverified.

Difficulty is explicitly partial: `clamp(1 + distance_m/5000 + 0.12*intersection_count + min(3, uncertain_m/250), 1, 10)`. Elevation, gradients, terrain risk and unmapped barriers are unknown. No score is issued when no mission route is found.

## Planned maximum-deviation grading

`grading.py` classifies the mission route's unrounded `max_deviation_m`, including the existing 0.05 m conservative guard: Diamond `<10`, Platinum `<25`, Gold `<50`, Silver `<75`, Bronze `≤100`, otherwise Out of grade. Each check is evaluated in that order. Exactly 10/25/50/75 m enters the next tier; exactly 100 m remains Bronze. Display rounding and imperial conversion do not affect classification. The numerical tolerance and source mapping accuracy still apply near cutoffs.

The API returns `planned_grade` separately from feasibility, access confidence and partial difficulty. A route beyond 100 m can remain corridor-compliant but out of grade. Missing data or no mission route yields `null`, even when an ideal line or walking comparison is available. Discovery uses the same analysis function. `GET /api/grading` supplies the authoritative scale to the UI. Loading older stored analyses calculates a grade from the saved mission maximum without altering their route geometry. Mission GeoJSON/GPX and JSON summary exports retain the estimate; ideal and walking route exports do not carry a mission grade.

Route statistics also include grouped `verification_sections`, each with a reason, geometry and representative map coordinate. Consecutive uncertain edges on the same way are grouped for the checklist. These fields travel with mission GeoJSON/JSON exports; GPX exports include a named waypoint and reason for each check, as well as target compliance and check counts in the track description. A tentative section contributes its proposed geometry to the planned deviation grade, which remains an estimate requiring verification.

Only the generated network path is assessed, excluding unassessed endpoint gaps as with other route statistics. No recorded traveler track is evaluated, so this feature does not certify a completed mission grade.

## Discovery

A ≤64 km² rectangle is downloaded once. Up to 100 mapped nodes are sampled deterministically, candidate pairs in the requested length range are shuffled, and at most 24 are analysed. Surface preference filters endpoint candidates by mapped tags; it does not constrain every intermediate segment or infer elevation. At most five UI results are retained (API supports ten). Ranking prefers within-target results, then mapped status, lower bottleneck, fewer mapped intersections and shorter distance. Cancellation stops between bounded candidates; an in-flight provider request or CPU candidate may complete. Jobs are process-local and expire after one hour when a later search starts.
